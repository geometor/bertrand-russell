Summary — Preface (1903)
========================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. v–ix) <pom-preface-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-preface-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

In the December 1902 Preface to :cite:`*The Principles of Mathematics* <russell:1903>`, :person:`Bertrand Russell <russell>` announces his decisive break from the neo-Kantian and neo-Hegelian framework of his 1897 :cite:`*Essay on the Foundations of Geometry* <russell:1897>`. Guided by :person:`G. E. Moore <moore>`'s analytic realism, :person:`Giuseppe Peano <peano>`'s symbolic logic, :person:`Georg Cantor <cantor>`'s transfinite arithmetic, and :person:`Gottlob Frege <frege>`'s foundational works, Russell formulates the twin pillars of logicism: (1) that all pure mathematics—including Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometry—derives solely from a small set of logical constants without appeal to spatial intuition, and (2) that mathematical existence-theorems are secured constructively through the principle of abstraction.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

Philosophical Realism of Propositions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Against psychologism and Kantian idealism, Russell holds that what is true or false—a *proposition*—is non-mental, objective, and composed of mind-independent terms and relations.

Intellectual Debt to Peano, Frege, and Whitehead
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Russell credits :person:`Peano <peano>`'s symbolic notation for separating distinct logical relations previously conflated by ordinary grammar (membership vs. inclusion), acknowledges independent convergence with :person:`Frege <frege>`, and highlights ongoing collaboration with :person:`A. N. Whitehead <whitehead>` toward Volume II (realized in 1910–1913 as :cite:`*Principia Mathematica* <whitehead-russell:1910>`).

Constructive Existence-Theorems
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Defining cardinals, ordinals, complex numbers, and geometric ideal elements as logical classes via equivalence relations eliminates unproved postulates and guarantees non-emptiness whenever the underlying relation is instantiated.
