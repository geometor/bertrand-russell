Summary — Chapter XLVII: Metrical Geometry (§§ 388–404)
=======================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 404–418) <pom-ch-47-metrical-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-47-metrical-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter XLVII (*Metrical Geometry*, §§388–404, pp. 404–418) is the celebrated locus classicus cited by both :person:`Fr. Peter Hoenen, S.J. <hoenen>` (:cite:`*De Noetica Geometriae*, Caput IV <hoenen:1954>`) and :person:`Sir Thomas L. Heath <heath>` (:cite:`*Euclid* Vol. I, pp. 227, 249 <euclid:elements:i-4>`). In **§§389–391 (pp. 404–407)**, :person:`Russell <russell>` delivers his incisive critique of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s tacit appeals to spatial intuition in Prop. ``I.1`` (two-circle intersection) and of the method of :term:`superposition <superposition>` in Prop. ``I.4`` ('strikes every intelligent child as a juggle'), exposing the logical circularity of defining spatial congruence through rigid material bodies (the thermometer by the window and fire) and formulating the exact three-step congruence postulate required to replace motion. Sections 392–404 then build metrical geometry rigorously from :term:`stretches <stretch>`, :term:`magnitude of divisibility <magnitude-of-divisibility>`, and angles defined as stretches of rays.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§389 (pp. 404–405): Critique of Euclid I.1 and Implicit Intuition
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Russell shows that ``I.1`` assumes without proof that two coplanar circles whose centers lie on each other's circumference intersect, and ``I.4``, ``I.7``, ``I.16`` rely on unaxiomatized planar order (*inside/outside*) and betweenness.

§390 (pp. 405–406): Why Superposition Fails Logically
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

First, spatial points are immutable positions ('can no more change its position than the leopard can change his spots'). Second, moving material triangles requires *rigidity*, whose very definition presupposes prior spatial equality—illustrated by setting up $\triangle ABC$ by the window and $\triangle DEF$ by the fire, where a heated thermometer changes equally with the triangles.

§391 (pp. 406–407): The Congruence Postulate Replacing Superposition
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Following :person:`Hilbert <hilbert>`, Russell states the exact triangle-determination and SAS congruence axioms needed to validate ``I.4``, ``I.8``, and ``I.26`` without motion, while noting that ``I.16`` fails in spherical/elliptic space unless open serial order holds.

§§396–402: Magnitude of Divisibility and Angle as Stretch of Rays
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Distances are derived from the :term:`magnitude of divisibility <magnitude-of-divisibility>` of generating serial relations rather than point-set cardinality ($2^{\alpha_0}$); angles must be defined as stretches of rays rather than unbounded planar sectors (§402), because in hyperbolic space a proper sub-sector could otherwise exceed the whole.
