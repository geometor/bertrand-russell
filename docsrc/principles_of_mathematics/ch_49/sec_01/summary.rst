Summary — Definitions of Various Spaces as Classes of Relations
===============================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Section Track <pom-ch_49-sec_01-en>` · *Critical Notes:* :ref:`Notes Track <pom-ch_49-sec_01-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter XLIX (*Definitions of Various Spaces*, §§412–415, pp. 429–436) demonstrates the culminating power of the logicist method in geometry: instead of treating 'Points', 'Lines', and 'Axioms' as unproved primitive postulates about actual space, :person:`Russell <russell>` turns every geometry into an explicit nominal *definition* of a class of relational structures—defining **projective spaces** (§413), **Euclidean spaces** via Peano–Whitehead directed magnitudes/vectors (§414), and **Clifford's locally flat finite spaces** (§415), and proving existence-theorems for each from the arithmetic continuum of real numbers.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§412: Elimination of Geometric Axioms in Pure Mathematics
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In pure geometry there are no geometric axioms—only the nominal definition of a class of spaces (e.g. 'Euclidean spaces') together with an arithmetic existence proof built from ordered triples of real numbers, demonstrating consistency once for all.

§414: Vectorial Definition of Euclidean Space
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Euclidean :math:`n`-space is compactly defined as the field of a group of everywhere-defined bijective directed magnitudes (vectors) forming an :math:`n`-dimensional real inner-product space.

§415: Clifford's Spaces and Klein's Space-Form Problem
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Examining :person:`Clifford <clifford>`'s cylindrical/flat manifolds of finite extent where angle-sum equals :math:`\pi` everywhere yet straight lines close, Russell highlights the distinction between local and global topological axioms.
