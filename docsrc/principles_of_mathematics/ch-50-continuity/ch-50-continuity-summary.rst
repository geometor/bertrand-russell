Summary — Chapter L: The Continuity of Space (§§ 416–422)
=========================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 437–444) <pom-ch-50-continuity-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-50-continuity-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter L (*The Continuity of Space*, §§416–422, pp. 437–444) integrates :person:`Cantor <cantor>`'s ordinal continuity (Part V) into projective and metrical geometry. :person:`Russell <russell>` formulates projective continuity via Dedekind's axiom on harmonic quadrants (§416), metrical continuity via Archimedes and Dedekind cuts (§417), notes that continuity suffices to prove :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s circle-intersection existence theorems though Hilbert showed a weaker algebraic field suffices (§418), critiques empirical geometry (§420), and concludes against monistic tradition that continuous space is literally an infinite aggregate of points (§422).

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§418–419: Circle Postulate vs. Full Continuity
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Continuity implies the intersection of lines and circles assumed tacitly in Euclid ``I.1``, yet :person:`Hilbert <hilbert>` proved that Euclidean compass-and-straightedge constructions require only a countable quadratic-surd subfield (`GEOMETOR`'s exact algebraic domain!).

§420: Critique of Approximate Empirical Geometry
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Anticipating Hoenen's problem of exactitude from the opposite direction, Russell remarks that sense-perception yields only finite margins of error, whereas pure geometry treats exact hypothetical spaces.

§422: Space as an Infinite Aggregate of Points
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Rejecting the Aristotelian–Kantian dictum that points are mere limits of division, Russell insists that Cantor's set theory rehabilitates space as a literal class of $2^{\alpha_0}$ indivisible points.
