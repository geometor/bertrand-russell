Summary — Chapter XLVI: Descriptive Geometry (§§ 374–387)
=========================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 393–403) <pom-ch-46-descriptive-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-46-descriptive-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter XLVI (*Descriptive Geometry*, §§374–387, pp. 393–403) examines spaces in which two points determine an *open* serial line rather than a closed projective circuit—the common ordinal substrate of Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry prior to congruence. Synthesizing :person:`Moritz Pasch <pasch>` (:cite:`1882 <pasch:1882>`) and :person:`Giuseppe Peano <peano>` (:cite:`1889/1894 <peano:1889>`), :person:`Russell <russell>` axiomatizes :term:`betweenness <betweenness>`, proves planar intersection properties via Pasch's transversal axiom (§380), and demonstrates how adjoining :term:`ideal points, lines, and planes <ideal-elements>` defined as logical classes (sheaves of lines) embeds every descriptive three-space into a projective space (§§383–387).

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§375–378: Pasch–Peano Betweenness vs. Serial Relations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Lines may be generated either from Peano's primitive open segment $ab$ ($c \in ab$) or from a class $K$ of transitive asymmetrical ray-relations $R$ with converse $\breve{R}$.

§380: Pasch's Axiom and the Descriptive Plane
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A plane cannot be defined merely as lines joining $A$ to points of $BC$, because parallels or hyperbolic ultra-parallels leave gaps; Pasch's triangle-transversal postulates close the plane.

§§383–387: Logical Construction of Ideal Elements
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Instead of feigning non-existent 'points at infinity', Pasch and Russell define an *ideal point* as the class (sheaf) of coplanar ray-pairs satisfying three-plane concurrence—securing the existence-theorem purely logically.
