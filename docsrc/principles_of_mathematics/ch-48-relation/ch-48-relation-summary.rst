Summary — Chapter XLVIII: Relation of Metrical to Projective and Descriptive Geometry (§§ 405–411)
==================================================================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 419–428) <pom-ch-48-relation-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-48-relation-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter XLVIII (*Relation of Metrical to Projective and Descriptive Geometry*, §§405–411, pp. 419–428) examines the :person:`Cayley <cayley>`–:person:`Klein <klein>` projective theory of distance and reverses Russell's own 1897 assessment. Whereas in 1897 Russell dismissed projective metrics as 'merely technical' because true distance seemed philosophically quantitative, in 1903 he shows that on pure logicist principles *any* two relations satisfying the axioms of distance are mathematically indistinguishable: defining distance via logarithmic functions of :term:`anharmonic ratios <anharmonic-ratio>` with respect to :term:`the Absolute <the-absolute>` (§§407–409) provides a completely rigorous deduction of metrical properties inside projective or descriptive space.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§405–407: Why Projective Distance Is Logically Legitimate
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Once pure geometry deals only with formal properties of relations, a relation constructed from projective cross-ratios that obeys every axiom of distance *is* a valid distance relation.

§§408–409: Classification of Spaces via the Absolute Quadric
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

An imaginary non-degenerate quadric yields elliptic space; the interior of a real quadric yields hyperbolic space; a degenerate imaginary conic in an ideal plane yields Euclidean space.
