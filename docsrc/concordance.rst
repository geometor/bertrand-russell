CROSS-CORPUS CONCORDANCE
========================

.. rubric:: Bidirectional Loci Across Russell (1897 & 1903), Hoenen (*De Noetica Geometriae*, 1954), Heath's *Euclid* (1908), and *Pappus*

This master concordance maps the foundational geometric arguments of :person:`Bertrand Russell <russell>` across his two Cambridge treatises—:cite:`*An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry* (1897) <russell:1897>` and :cite:`*The Principles of Mathematics* (1903) <russell:1903>`—directly to the classical and philosophical corpora in the **GEOMETOR** initiative: :person:`Fr. Petrus Hoenen, S.J. <hoenen>`'s Thomistic critique in :cite:`*De Noetica Geometriae* (1954) <hoenen:1954>`, :person:`Sir Thomas L. Heath <heath>`'s commentary on :cite:`*Euclid's Elements* (1908) <euclid:elements:i-4>`, and :person:`Pappus of Alexandria <pappus>`'s projective cross-ratio lemmas in :cite:`*Collection* Book VII <pappus:collection:vii>`.

1. Critique of Superposition & Motion in Geometry (Euclid I.4)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **1903 Principles of Mathematics:** :ref:`§ 390 <pom-390>` (pp. 405–406) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <pom-ch-47-metrical-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-47-metrical-notes>`
* **1897 Foundations of Geometry:** :ref:`§ 146 <efg-146>`, :ref:`§ 147 <efg-147>`, :ref:`§ 149 <efg-149>`, :ref:`§ 150 <efg-150>` (pp. 151–156) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <efg-ch-03b-metrical-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-03b-metrical-notes>`

.. pull-quote::

   "The fourth proposition is the first in which Euclid employs the method of superposition—a method which, since he will make any détour to avoid it, he evidently dislikes, and rightly, since it has no logical validity, and strikes every intelligent child as a juggle. In the first place, to speak of motion implies that our triangles are not spatial, but material. For a point of space is a position, and can no more change its position than the leopard can change his spots."
   — *The Principles of Mathematics* (1903), pp. 405–406

**Cross-Corpus Relations:**

* **geometor/hoenen** — *De Noetica Geometriae (1954)*, ``Caput IV, §2, pp. 107–111, nn. 3–5``: Hoenen quotes PoM §390 (pp. 405–406) verbatim and counters that geometry treats real extended being (ens extensum) rather than fictitious empty space; mobility is a strict proprium of extension as materia intelligibilis.
* **geometor/euclid** — *The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements (Heath, 1908)*, ``Vol. I, Book I, C.N. 4 (pp. 225–227) & Prop. I.4 (p. 249)``: Heath cites Russell on motion in geometry as transference of attention and on taking I.4 as an axiom.
* **euclid-elements** — *Euclid's Elements*, ``I.cn.4, I.4, I.8`` (:just:`I.cn.4`): Classical superposition proofs of triangle congruence.

2. Rigid Bodies, the Thermometer Paradox, and the Three-Step Congruence Axiom
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **1903 Principles of Mathematics:** :ref:`§ 390 <pom-390>`, :ref:`§ 391 <pom-391>` (pp. 405–407) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <pom-ch-47-metrical-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-47-metrical-notes>`
* **1897 Foundations of Geometry:** :ref:`§ 69 <efg-69>`, :ref:`§ 70 <efg-70>`, :ref:`§ 71 <efg-71>`, :ref:`§ 72 <efg-72>`, :ref:`§ 73 <efg-73>` (pp. 74–81) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <efg-ch-02-critique-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-02-critique-notes>`

.. pull-quote::

   "Suppose that the triangle ABC is by the window, and the side AB consists of the column of mercury in a thermometer; suppose also that DEF is by the fire. Let us apply ABC to DEF as Euclid directs, and let AB just cover DE. Then we are to conclude that ABC and DEF, before the motion, were equal in all respects."
   — *The Principles of Mathematics* (1903), pp. 405–407

**Cross-Corpus Relations:**

* **geometor/hoenen** — *De Noetica Geometriae (1954)*, ``Caput IV, §3, pp. 111–116, nn. 8–10``: Hoenen endorses Russell's refutation of Helmholtz/Poincaré physical-rigidity empiricism (including the thermometer illustration), then adapts Russell's §391 congruence postulate into a static proof of Euclid I.4 grounded in extension as principle of individuation.
* **hilbert** — *Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899)*, ``Axiom Group III (Congruence, III.1–III.5)``: Hilbert's axiomatic replacement of superposition by segment and angle congruence postulates, culminating in III.5 (SAS).
* **euclid-elements** — *Euclid's Elements*, ``I.4, I.16, I.23, I.26`` (:just:`I.4`): Triangle congruence theorems and exterior-angle theorem analyzed in PoM §391.

3. Circle Intersection in Euclid I.1, Spatial Intuition, and the Continuity Postulate
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **1903 Principles of Mathematics:** :ref:`§ 389 <pom-389>`, :ref:`§ 418 <pom-418>`, :ref:`§ 419 <pom-419>`, :ref:`§ 420 <pom-420>` (pp. 404–405, 439–442) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <pom-ch-47-metrical-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-47-metrical-notes>`
* **1897 Foundations of Geometry:** :ref:`§ 160 <efg-160>`, :ref:`§ 161 <efg-161>` (pp. 163–165) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <efg-ch-03b-metrical-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-03b-metrical-notes>`

.. pull-quote::

   "To begin with the first proposition. There is no evidence whatever that the circles which we are told to construct intersect, and if they do not, the whole proposition fails. Euclid’s problems are often regarded as existence-theorems, and from this point of view, it is plain, the assumption that the circles in question intersect is precisely the same as the assumption that there is an equilateral triangle on a given base."
   — *The Principles of Mathematics* (1903), pp. 404–405, 439–442

**Cross-Corpus Relations:**

* **geometor/hoenen** — *De Noetica Geometriae (1954)*, ``Caput IV, §7, pp. 139–142, n. 28``: Hoenen quotes PoM §389 (p. 404) at length and distinguishes demonstrative deduction (scientia) from intuitive grasp of first principles (intellectus), arguing that continuous planar separation makes circle intersection an intelligible necessity rather than a logical fallacy.
* **geometor/euclid** — *The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements (Heath, 1908)*, ``Vol. I, Book I, Prop. I.1 commentary (pp. 242–243)``: Dedekind–Pasch–Hilbert continuity analysis of two-circle intersection in Proposition I.1.
* **euclid-elements** — *Euclid's Elements*, ``I.post.3, I.1`` (:just:`I.post.3`): Construction of an equilateral triangle on a given finite straight line via two intersecting circles.

10. Russell's Philosophical Turn: From 1897 Neo-Kantian Form of Externality to 1903 Logicism
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **1903 Principles of Mathematics:** :ref:`§ 351 <pom-351>`, :ref:`§ 352 <pom-352>`, :ref:`§ 356 <pom-356>`, :ref:`§ 412 <pom-412>`, :ref:`§ 413 <pom-413>`, :ref:`§ 432 <pom-432>`, :ref:`§ 436 <pom-436>` (pp. 371–376, 429–433, 454–461) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <pom-ch-44-dimensions-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <pom-ch-44-dimensions-notes>`
* **1897 Foundations of Geometry:** :ref:`§ 103 <efg-103>`, :ref:`§ 132 <efg-132>`, :ref:`§ 139 <efg-139>`, :ref:`§ 186 <efg-186>`, :ref:`§ 192 <efg-192>`, :ref:`§ 209 <efg-209>` (pp. 117, 139–146, 182–201) — :ref:`Chapter Unit <efg-ch-03a-projective-en>` · :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-03a-projective-notes>`

.. pull-quote::

   "As a branch of pure mathematics, Geometry is strictly deductive, indifferent to the choice of its premisses and to the question whether there exist (in the strict sense) such entities as its premisses define. Many different and even inconsistent sets of premisses lead to propositions which would be called geometrical, but all such sets have a common element. This element is wholly summed up by the statement that Geometry deals with series of more than one dimension."
   — *The Principles of Mathematics* (1903), pp. 371–376, 429–433, 454–461

**Cross-Corpus Relations:**

* **geometor/bertrand-russell** — *Internal Comparative Evolution (1897 vs. 1903)*, ``EFG Preface & Chs. III–IV vs. PoM Preface, Ch. I, Chs. XLIV, XLIX, LI–LII``: In 1897 Russell deduced projective geometry transcendentally from the 'form of externality' and ended in Hegelian antinomies of the point; after encountering Moore, Peano, Cantor, and Pieri (1898–1900), he redefined pure spaces in 1903 as purely logical classes of serial relations.
