Summary — Introduction: Our Problem Defined (§§ 1–9)
====================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 1–6) <efg-intro-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-intro-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

The Introduction (*Our Problem Defined by Its Relations to Logic, Psychology and Mathematics*, §§1–9, pp. 1–6) severs :person:`Kant <kant>`'s identification of the *a priori* with the psychologically *subjective*. :person:`Russell <russell>` defines a postulate as logically **a priori** if and only if without it experience of the science's subject-matter would be impossible (§§3–5), and outlines his central thesis (§9): :term:`projective geometry <projective-geometry>` and the general axioms of :term:`free mobility <free-mobility>`, manifold continuity, and multiple dimensions are *a priori* conditions of any :term:`form of externality <form-of-externality>`, whereas the specific Euclidean zero-curvature value and exact triad of dimensions are empirical.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§1–5: Epistemology Freed from Empirical Psychology
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Whether space perception is innate or learned belongs to psychology; epistemology asks purely logically what axioms are presupposed in the possibility of experiencing outer diversity.

§9: Partition of Geometric Axioms
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Axioms shared by Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical/elliptic spaces are *a priori*; axioms distinguishing Euclid (zero space-constant and $n = 3$) are empirical.
