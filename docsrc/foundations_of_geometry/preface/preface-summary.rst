Summary — Preface & Dedication (1897)
=====================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. v–vi) <efg-preface-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-preface-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Written at Haslemere in May 1897 and dedicated to Cambridge idealist John McTaggart Ellis McTaggart, the Preface to :cite:`*An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry* <russell:1897>` introduces :person:`Bertrand Russell <russell>`'s revised 1895 Trinity College Fellowship dissertation. Russell credits :person:`Felix Klein <klein>`'s lectures on non-Euclidean geometry for the three-period historical framework of Chapter I, F. H. Bradley, Sigwart, and Bosanquet in logic, and :person:`A. N. Whitehead <whitehead>` for awakening him to the decisive philosophical primacy of :term:`projective geometry <projective-geometry>`.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

Synthesis of Klein's Mathematics and Post-Kantian Epistemology
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Russell bridges Continental differential/projective geometry—hitherto largely ignored by British philosophers—with critical epistemology.

Whitehead's Catalyst
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Whitehead's insight that projective geometry captures the qualitative structure common to Euclidean and non-Euclidean spaces becomes the linchpin of Chapter III, Section A.
