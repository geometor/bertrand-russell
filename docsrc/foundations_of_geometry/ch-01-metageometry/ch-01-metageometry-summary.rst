Summary — Chapter I: A Short History of Metageometry (§§ 10–50)
===============================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 7–53) <efg-ch-01-metageometry-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-01-metageometry-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter I (*A Short History of Metageometry*, §§10–50, pp. 7–53) presents a masterly historical and mathematical analysis of nineteenth-century :term:`Metageometry <metageometry>` across three periods (following :person:`Klein <klein>`): **First Period** (§§12–15: :person:`Gauss <gauss>`, :person:`Lobachevsky <lobatchewsky>`, :person:`János Bolyai <bolyai>`), establishing synthetic consistency without the parallel postulate; **Second Period** (§§16–29: :person:`Riemann <riemann>`, :person:`Helmholtz <helmholtz>`, :person:`Beltrami <beltrami>`), analyzing space as a quantitative :term:`manifold <manifold>` of constant :term:`measure of curvature <measure-of-curvature>`; and **Third Period** (§§30–45: :person:`Cayley <cayley>`, :person:`Klein <klein>`, :person:`Sophus Lie <lie>`), subordinating metrical geometry to projective invariance relative to :term:`the Absolute <the-absolute>`.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§17–26: Riemann's Curvature and Helmholtz's Free Mobility
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Riemann's geodesic 2-pencils (Fig. 1, p. 21) show that figures move without distortion if and only if Gaussian curvature $k$ is constant everywhere.

§§30–43: Cayley–Klein Projective Metrics and Imaginaries
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Russell praises von Staudt's purely projective cross-ratio (§36) and Beltrami–Ball models (§§38–39), examines non-orientability of the elliptic plane (Fig. 2, p. 41), and argues in 1897 that Cayley's reduction of distance and imaginary loci is technically valid but philosophically conventional (a verdict he reverses in :ref:`PoM Ch. XLVIII <pom-406>`).

§45: Sophus Lie's Group-Theoretic Resolution
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Lie's continuous transformation groups prove Helmholtz's separate axiom of Monodromy superfluous in three dimensions.
