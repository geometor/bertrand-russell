Summary — Chapter III, Section A: The Axioms of Projective Geometry (§§ 102–140)
================================================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 117–147) <efg-ch-03a-projective-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-03a-projective-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter III, Section A (*The Axioms of Projective Geometry*, §§102–140, pp. 117–147) presents :person:`Russell <russell>`'s constructive philosophical deduction of :term:`projective geometry <projective-geometry>` as wholly *a priori*. Expounding qualitative projective equivalence, :term:`anharmonic ratio <anharmonic-ratio>` (Fig. 6, p. 123), :term:`harmonic ranges <harmonic-range>` (Fig. 7, p. 124), and :person:`von Staudt <staudt>`'s :term:`quadrilateral construction <quadrilateral-construction>` (Fig. 8, p. 125; §§111–114), Russell isolates three implicit projective axioms—homogeneity/relativity of position, unique determination of lines and planes (Fig. 9, p. 145), and finite multi-dimensionality—and deduces them from the general concept of a :term:`form of externality <form-of-externality>` (§§128–139).

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§111–114: Von Staudt's Purely Qualitative Harmonic Fourth
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Projective cross-ratio requires no measurement of segments: harmonic conjugation is defined purely by incidence via the complete quadrilateral $ABDOPQRS T$ (Fig. 8, p. 125), whose uniqueness follows from Desargues's theorem in three-space.

§§128–139: Deduction from the Form of Externality
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Any medium enabling experience of simultaneous diverse interrelated things must be purely relational, homogeneous, infinitely divisible, and of at least two (and finite) dimensions, entailing two-point line determination and three-point plane closure (Footnote 144, Fig. 9).
