An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry¶

Complete 1897 Cambridge University Press First Edition (Preface, Dedication, and §§ 1–209)

AN ESSAY ON THE FOUNDATIONS OF GEOMETRY
BY BERTRAND A. W. RUSSELL, M.A.
FELLOW OF TRINITY COLLEGE, CAMBRIDGE
CAMBRIDGE: AT THE UNIVERSITY PRESS — 1897
To John McTaggart Ellis McTaggart, to whose discourse and friendship is owing the existence of this book.

Editorial Scope & Diagram Reconstructions¶

This Book Codex reproduces the complete, unabridged 1897 Cambridge University Press first edition of An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry (xvi + 201 pp., all 209 numbered sections §§ 1–209 and all 171 authorial footnotes), accompanied by 9 exact analytical vector SVG diagrams newly reconstructed from Russell’s geometric specifications—including von Staudt’s complete quadrilateral harmonic construction (Fig. 8, p. 125, § 113).

Chapters¶

Preface & Dedication (1897)

Russell’s Cambridge Fellowship dissertation expansion investigating nineteenth-century metageometry and deducing the a priori axioms of projective and metrical space.

Introduction: Our Problem Defined

Russell defines the epistemological problem of geometry against psychologism, establishing a strictly logical criterion of the a priori as that which is necessary for experience.

Chapter I: A Short History of Metageometry

Russell’s historical survey tracing metageometry through its three classical periods: the synthetic (Gauss, Lobachevsky, Bolyai), the metrical (Riemann, Helmholtz, Beltrami), and the projective (Cayley, Klein, Lie).

Chapter II: Critical Account of Previous Philosophical Theories

A critical examination of the philosophical foundations of geometry across Kant, Riemann, Helmholtz, Erdmann, Lotze, and Delbœuf, dismantling empiricist misconceptions while preserving the transcendental necessity of a form of externality.

Chapter III: The Axioms of Geometry

Russell’s central deduction establishing both the qualitative axioms of projective geometry (straight line, plane, complete quadrilateral, duality) and the quantitative axioms of metrical geometry (Free Mobility, dimensions, distance).

Chapter IV: Philosophical Consequences

The philosophical synthesis confronting the antinomy of the point and resolving the conflict between the relativity of empty space and the necessity of matter for geometrical relations.
