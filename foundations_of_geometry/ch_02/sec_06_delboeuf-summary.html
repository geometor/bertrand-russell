Summary — Delbœuf and Recent French Geometrical Speculation¶

Primary Text: Read Verbatim Section Track · Critical Notes: Notes Track

Executive Summary¶

Chapter II (Critical Account of Some Previous Philosophical Theories of Geometry, §§51–101, pp. 54–116) subjects six major philosophers and scientist-philosophers to sustained mathematical and logical scrutiny: Kant (§§51–58), Riemann (§§59–65), Helmholtz (§§66–73), Erdmann (§§74–84), Lotze (§§85–96), and Delbœuf plus French conventionalists (§§97–101). Particularly notable are §§69–73, refuting Helmholtz’s claim that pure geometry depends on physical rigid bodies, and §§90–95, exposing the mathematical fallacies in Lotze’s attacks on parallels, spherical 2-space, and higher dimensions.

Key Arguments & Theses¶

§§53–58: Separating Kant’s Two Lines of Argument¶

Metageometry refutes Kant’s regressive argument from Euclidean apodeicticity, while Kant’s metaphysical deduction establishes only some homogeneous form of externality, not specifically Euclidean parallels.

§§69–73: Physics Presupposes Geometry (Against Helmholtz)¶

Defining congruence via physical rigid bodies is viciously circular because mechanics and rigidity cannot even be stated without prior spatial measurement—an argument echoed by Hoenen against Poincaré in Caput IV, §3.

§§90–95: Point-by-Point Refutation of Lotze (Figs. 3–5)¶

Lotze begs the parallel question in defining parallels $a\alpha, b\beta$ by equidistant chords (Fig. 3, p. 99), misconstrues intrinsic spherical geodesics $NBS$ (Fig. 4, p. 101), and ignores orders of infinity ($\infty^2$) in his perpendicularity proof of 3-space (Fig. 5, p. 106).
