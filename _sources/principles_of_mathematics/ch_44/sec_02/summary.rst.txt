Summary — Complex Numbers and Spatial Coordinate Systems
========================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Section Track <pom-ch_44-sec_02-en>` · *Critical Notes:* :ref:`Notes Track <pom-ch_44-sec_02-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Opening Part VI (*Space*), Chapter XLIV (*Dimensions and Complex Numbers*, §§351–360, pp. 371–380) recasts the concept of spatial dimension into pure relation-arithmetic. Reviewing the rise of non-Euclidean geometry (§353), :person:`Russell <russell>` defines geometry as the theory of multiple series of two or more dimensions (§352) and demonstrates against :person:`Cantor <cantor>`'s bijection paradox (:math:`2^{\alpha_0}`) that dimensionality belongs not to the cardinal point-set, but to the nested hierarchy of generating serial relations (§§354–356). Sections 357–360 apply this definition to universal algebra and :math:`n`-tuple complex numbers, proving Stolz's theorem that ordinary two-term complex numbers (:math:`x + iy`) are the maximal extension preserving the field laws.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§351–353: Retrospect on Non-Euclidean Geometry
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Summarizing :person:`Gauss <gauss>`, :person:`Lobachevsky <lobatchewsky>`, :person:`Bolyai <bolyai>`, :person:`Riemann <riemann>`, :person:`Beltrami <beltrami>`, and :person:`Cayley <cayley>`–:person:`Klein <klein>`, Russell shows how metageometry forced the separation of pure relational geometry from physical space.

§§354–356: Purely Logical Definition of :math:`n` Dimensions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

An :math:`n`-dimensional series is defined inductively through :math:`n` transitive asymmetrical relations mapping terms through nested classes of series, resolving Cantor's discovery that a line and a plane have the same cardinal number of points.

§§357–360: Complex Numbers as Ordered Couples of Reals
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Drawing on :person:`Whitehead <whitehead>`'s :cite:`*Universal Algebra* <whitehead:1898>`, Russell rejects formalist 'uninterpretable symbols' and defines complex numbers explicitly as one-dimensional series of real-number couples.
