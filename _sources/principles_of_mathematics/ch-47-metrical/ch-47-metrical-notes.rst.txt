Critical Notes — Chapter XLVII: Metrical Geometry (§§ 388–404)
==============================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 404–418) <pom-ch-47-metrical-en>` · *Overview:* :ref:`Analytical Summary <pom-ch-47-metrical-summary>` · *Cross-Corpus Loci:* :ref:`Master Concordance <concordance>`

Historical & Cross-Corpus Commentary
------------------------------------

Hoenen's Direct Rebuttal in De Noetica Geometriae (Caput IV, §§2–3 & §7)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

This chapter is the primary Russellian text engaged in ``geometor/hoenen``:

1. **Caput IV, §2, nn. 3–5 (pp. 107–111)** quotes :ref:`§390 (pp. 405–406) <pom-390>` verbatim ('strikes every intelligent child as a juggle... leopard can change its spots') and replies that Russell mistakes fictitious empty receptacle-space (*spatium imaginarium*) for real extended being (*ens extensum*), of which continuous mobility and homogeneous site-individuation (:term:`materia intelligibilis <materia-intelligibilis>`) are essential *propria*.
2. **Caput IV, §3 (pp. 111–116)** applauds Russell's thermometer refutation of Poincaré/Helmholtz physical rigidity, then converts Russell's :ref:`§391 <pom-391>` congruence postulate into an intuitive static demonstration from the homogeneity of extension.
3. **Caput IV, §7, n. 28 (pp. 139–142)** quotes :ref:`§389 (p. 404) <pom-389>` on ``I.1`` circle intersection, arguing that while formal *scientia* must list continuity explicitly, architectonic *intellectus principiorum* legitimately grasps circle-intersection from planar continuity.

Heath's Commentary on Euclid Book I (C.N. 4 & Prop. 4)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In ``geometor/euclid``, :person:`Sir Thomas L. Heath <heath>` (:cite:`Vol. I, p. 227 & p. 249 <euclid:elements:i-4>`) cites :ref:`PoM p. 405 (§§389–390) <pom-p405>` and Russell's :cite:`1902 *Britannica* article <russell:1902:britannica>` when concluding that Euclid used superposition reluctantly and that modern axiomatics rightly postulates ``I.4`` with Hilbert.
