Summary — Chapter III, Section B: The Axioms of Metrical Geometry (§§ 141–179)
==============================================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 147–177) <efg-ch-03b-metrical-en>` · *Commentary:* :ref:`Critical Notes <efg-ch-03b-metrical-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter III, Section B (*The Axioms of Metrical Geometry*, §§141–179, pp. 147–177) transitions from pure projective quality to spatial quantity and measurement, deriving three *a priori* metrical axioms presupposed in any measurable spatial manifold: **I. The Axiom of Free Mobility** (Congruence, §§146–161), **II. The Axiom of Dimensions** (finite integral dimensionality, with $n = 3$ empirical, §§162–166), and **III. The Axiom of Distance** (unique two-point magnitude along geodesics, with local Euclidean flatness as limiting calculus condition, §§167–176).

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§146–154: Philosophical Deduction of Free Mobility
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Spatial comparison of separated magnitudes requires motion without distortion; to hypothesize that shapes vary systematically with position (§147) is logically meaningless unless some position-independent standard existed outside space. Notice how close 1897 Russell is here to defending congruence via mobility—six years before repudiating superposition in :ref:`PoM §390 <pom-390>`!

§§149–153: Enantiomorphs (Chiral Solids), Time, and Visual Estimation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Russell resolves four objections to congruence: chiral solids (right/left hands), temporal measurement, optical estimation, and curved surfaces embedded in 3-space.

§§167–176: Unique Two-Point Distance and Spherical Exception
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Pairwise point-relations require unique distance; antipodal ambiguity in spherical space (§170–172) motivates preferring Klein's polar elliptic space or treating spherical geometry via embedded intuition.
