Summary — The Antinomy of the Point and Geometrical Order
=========================================================

*Primary Text:* :ref:`Read Verbatim Section Track <efg-ch_04-sec_02-en>` · *Critical Notes:* :ref:`Notes Track <efg-ch_04-sec_02-notes>`

Executive Summary
-----------------

Chapter IV (*Philosophical Consequences*, §§180–209, pp. 178–201) completes the 1897 *Essay* in two movements: first (§§181–192), proving against monism and pure temporalism that experience of diverse interrelated things requires a multi-dimensional :term:`form of externality <form-of-externality>` given in sense-perception; and second (§§193–209), diagnosing three Hegelian **antinomies of pure space**—the **Antinomy of the Point**, the **Circle in Relational Figures**, and **Relativity vs. Homogeneity**—concluding that geometry can escape contradiction only by referring spatial relations to unextended material atoms, pointing onward to kinematics and dynamics.

Key Arguments & Theses
----------------------

§§186–191: Why Time Alone Cannot Ground Diverse Things
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A one-dimensional temporal series yields only successive adjectives of a single subject; simultaneous substantival diversity requires a multi-dimensional form of externality.

§§194–205: The Three Antinomies of Empty Relational Space
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Taken in abstraction from matter, points are neither extended figures nor unextended zeroes, and spatial relations appear to be 'relations without terms'—a dialectical impasse Russell solves provisionally in 1897 by positing unextended material atoms as terms.
