Summary — Chapter XLV: Projective Geometry (§§ 361–373)¶

Primary Text: Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 381–392) · Commentary: Critical Notes

Executive Summary¶

Chapter XLV (Projective Geometry, §§361–373, pp. 381–392) establishes the pure qualitative architecture of projective three-space without appeal to betweenness, segments, or metrical distance. Following Karl Georg Christian von Staudt (1847) and Mario Pieri (1898), Russell defines lines and planes as classes of points determined by symmetric incidence relations, constructs harmonic ranges via the complete quadrilateral (§365), derives cyclic order via Vailati’s separation of couples and involutions (§§366–367), assigns rational and real projective coordinates through Möbius nets (§§368–371), and culminates in projective duality (§373).

Key Arguments & Theses¶

§§361–362: Projective, Descriptive, and Metrical Geometry¶

Russell classifies modern foundational research into three autonomous tiers: projective (closed lines, incidence only), descriptive (open serial lines, betweenness), and metrical (quantitative congruence of stretches and angles).

§365: Von Staudt’s Complete Quadrilateral Construction¶

Given collinear points $a, b, c$, their harmonic fourth $d$ is constructed solely by joining and intersecting lines in three-space; Desargues’s theorem guarantees uniqueness independent of the auxiliary plane and projection centers.

§§367–371: Projective Generation of Order and Coordinates¶

Harmonic separation generates Vailati’s four-term cyclic order on every projective line, enabling Möbius’s harmonic net to map points bijectively to real coordinates and define anharmonic ratio non-metrically.
