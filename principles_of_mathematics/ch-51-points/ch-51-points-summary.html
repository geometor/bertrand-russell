Summary — Chapter LI: Logical Arguments Against Points (§§ 423–431)¶

Primary Text: Read Verbatim Treatise Track (pp. 445–453) · Commentary: Critical Notes

Executive Summary¶

Chapter LI (Logical Arguments Against Points, §§423–431, pp. 445–453) confronts the classical metaphysical controversy between absolute (substantival) and relational theories of space. Reversing the relational conclusion of his 1897 Essay (Ch. IV), Russell dissects Lotze’s monadistic critique of empty space (§§424–428) and Leibniz’s identity of indiscernibles (§429), arguing that relationalism covertly presupposes irreducible asymmetric spatial relations whose terms can only be absolute, unextended points differing immediately without qualitative adjectives.

Key Arguments & Theses¶

§§424–428: Dissection of Lotze’s Metaphysik¶

Against Lotze’s claim that empty points have neither ‘being’ nor ‘validity’ and dissolve into relations, Russell applies Moore’s distinction between timeless being (subsistence of all logical entities) and spatio-temporal existence.

§§429–431: Failure of Pure Material Relationalism¶

Because spatial relations are complex and continuous while material particles may be sparse or absent from unoccupied positions, geometry requires points themselves—differing numerically prior to concepts—as the ultimate terms of spatial relations.
