THE PRINCIPLES OF MATHEMATICS¶

Vol. I — 1903 First Edition: Preface, Part I (Chapter I), and Part VI (Space, Chapters XLIV–LII)

THE PRINCIPLES OF MATHEMATICS
BY BERTRAND RUSSELL, M.A.
LATE FELLOW OF TRINITY COLLEGE, CAMBRIDGE
VOL. I
CAMBRIDGE: AT THE UNIVERSITY PRESS — 1903

Editorial Scope & Public-Domain Provenance¶

This Book Codex presents the unabridged, verbatim 1903 Cambridge University Press first edition of the foundational and geometrical portions of The Principles of Mathematics: the 1903 Preface (pp. v–ix), Part I, Chapter I (Definition of Pure Mathematics, §§ 1–10, pp. 3–9), and the complete Part VI: Space (Chapters XLIV–LII, §§ 351–436, pp. 371–461)—comprising 96 numbered sections in full, including Chapter XLVII (§§ 389–391, pp. 404–407) where Russell formulates his celebrated critique of Euclid’s circle-intersection assumption (I.1) and method of superposition (I.4) directly answered by Fr. Petrus Hoenen, S.J. in De Noetica Geometriae (Caput IV) and cited by Sir Thomas L. Heath in The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements (Vol. I, pp. 225–227, 249).

Note

Russell’s later Introduction to the 1937 George Allen & Unwin second edition remains under copyright in several jurisdictions and is strictly excluded from this edition.
